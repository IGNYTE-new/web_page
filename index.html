The Attention Tax: Why Every Enquiry Costs You

Every time your phone buzzes with a new lead notification, something happens in your brain. You context-switch. You evaluate. You decide whether to act now or later. That cognitive load has a cost — even if you don't respond.

The Hidden Cost of Equal Treatment

Most dealerships treat all enquiries the same: they land in the inbox, someone scrolls through, and judgment happens manually. But that process is exhausting — and it doesn't scale. When your 40th notification looks identical to your 4th, your brain starts to tune out.

"The problem isn't that you're missing leads. It's that you're spending attention on leads that don't deserve it — and running dry when the real buyers arrive."

The "I need to think about it" Crusher

Use this when a customer goes ghost after getting the finance quote.

Act as a sympathetic but persistent car sales expert. Write a short, 2-sentence follow-up email to a customer who stopped replying after receiving a finance quote for a [Insert Car Model]. Acknowledge it's a big decision, but subtly remind them that this stock moves fast. End with a low-pressure question about their preferred monthly budget.
Inbound Response

The "Is this still available?" Reply

Turn a generic Facebook marketplace query into a phone call.

Write a response to a Facebook Marketplace enquiry asking "Is this available?". 1. Confirm yes. 2. Create urgency (mention 2 other viewings). 3. Ask "Are you looking to finance or cash?" to qualify them immediately. Keep it under 50 words. Informal tone.
Negotiation

The Low-Ball Rebuttal

How to handle a customer offering £2k below asking price.

Write a polite but firm email responding to a customer offering significantly less than the listed price. Explain that our cars are priced competitively based on the current UK market average (AutoTrader Retail Rating). Offer a small non-monetary incentive (like a full tank of fuel or extended warranty) instead of a discount.
